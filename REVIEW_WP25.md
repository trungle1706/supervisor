## 2. WP25b improvement plan (no torch, no retrain in this review)

Where the misses are now (held-out, balanced preset):

* `desc_inj.en` + `desc_inj.vi` cause 73 of the 198 misses (37%).
* `desc_ben.vi` causes 36 of the 59 false alarms (61%).
* Tool poisoning has the worst separation: recall 5133 bp, precision 6814 bp.
* Direct-argument misses (4666 bp) are spread across many templates: paraphrases with no cue words.
* Evasions: ROT13 1555, reversed 2888 and typo 4133 bp are the lowest.

| # | Change | Expected effect, and why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Per-surface models and calibration.** Surface-tagged copies of the concept features (`desc|OVR>RUL`), a separate weight block for `tool_description`, thresholds chosen per surface on dev (integer lookup table) | **Largest single gain.** One shared vector with a per-surface bias cannot learn that "must never" is normal in a description and suspicious in a memo. Descriptions cause 37% of the misses and 61% of the false alarms. |
| 2 | **Instruction-form cue features** that do not depend on the words: an imperative at the start of a clause (EN verb first; VI `hãy`, `đừng`, `không được`, `phải`), addressing the assistant in the second person (`you`, `bạn`, `em`), references to the conversation itself (`above`, `previous`, `trên`, `trước đó`), target-shift words (`instead`, `thay vào đó`), a modal followed by a money verb | **Second largest.** The direct misses are paraphrases that avoid the lexicon. These cues describe the speech act, not the wording, so they survive a template change. |
| 3 | **Word and subword tokens on normalised text**: VI syllable bigrams, a light EN stemmer (`ignoring` -> `ignor`), hashed with a surface tag. **Length-normalised score**: normalise the concept sum per window as well, and add a count feature. | Medium gain under template shift. Stems generalise across inflections. Normalising both sums reduces the dilution from long windows. |
| 4 | **Two-stage vote**: stage A is a fuzzy cue lexicon (Damerau distance 1 for words of 5 or more letters, after leet / Telex folding) that gives a cue score; stage B is the n-gram logit; a small stacked logistic model over (A, B, surface) is fitted with cross-fitting on train folds | Raises typo recall (4133 bp) and recall on paraphrases that keep one cue word. |
| 5 | **Decoding by plausibility**: score the ROT13 or reversed variant when the plain text has a low share of common VI / EN words (a list of about 2k words) and the decoded text has a high share. Today the decoded text must contain two words from the trigger lexicon. | ROT13 1555 and reversed 2888 bp should approach the base64 level (6000 bp). Today a paraphrase without lexicon words never triggers decoding. |
| 6 | **Training-set changes**: at least 300 independent template strings per pool, split by **family** (intent x structure), not by string. Counter-examples aimed at the false alarms: honest descriptions with `must`, `never` or `không được`, policy quotes, complaints. A paraphrase round with the instruction "no cue words". Evasion augmentation of the **train** pool only. | Raises the ceiling of every item above. Splitting by family makes dev measure the shift that held-out measures. |
| 7 | **Long-text selection by score** instead of by cue count: a cheap first-stage score (items 4 or 2) over every segment, and a cache of features per segment hash | Closes F12 (decoys), and cuts latency toward the 2 to 5 ms target |
| 8 | **Model class within the rules**: NBSVM (log-count-ratio features), stronger L2 (train fit is 10000 bp, which is overfit), bagging over template families; all in pure Python | A small, reliable gain in robustness, at no runtime cost apart from the ensemble size |

**Honest evaluation protocol for WP25b**

1. Seal `heldout.jsonl`, `evasion.jsonl` and `adversarial.jsonl` of today as "heldout-v1". Put their hashes in MANIFEST, and add a test that the trainer and any tuning script never open them.
2. Tune only with grouped k-fold over template families inside train + dev, and report the mean and spread over the folds.
3. Declare the targets and the threshold-selection rule in a commit before the final run.
4. Retrain on the committed files as version 1.1.0, so that the card hashes match the repo (closes F6).
5. Run on heldout-v1 **once**. Report Wilson intervals and a bootstrap that resamples **templates**, not items.
6. After that run, heldout-v1 becomes dev. The next held-out must come from another author or from bank red-team traffic.

Realistic expectation: items 1, 2 and 5 move recall most. Held-out balanced recall can rise from 5600 bp to about 7000 bp at 500 bp or less false alarm. 8500 bp under template shift is unlikely without real labelled data (owner question 1).
